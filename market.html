2-1. Why is Decarbonization Needed?

Global warming is no longer a "future problem"

In recent years, the effects of climate change have become visible around the world. Record-breaking heat waves, intensifying typhoons and hurricanes, glacial melting, and rising sea levels — these are no longer distant threats but realities unfolding right now.

The primary cause is the dramatic increase in greenhouse gases (GHGs) — particularly CO₂ — since the Industrial Revolution. The global average temperature has already risen approximately 1.1°C above pre-industrial levels, and without drastic action, it is projected to exceed 1.5°C by the mid-2030s.

🌡️ If warming exceeds 1.5°C, scientists warn of irreversible climate tipping points: coral reef collapse, permafrost thawing, accelerated ice sheet loss, and more.

Extreme Weather Events Affecting Japan

🌊

Intensifying Typhoons

The frequency of powerful typhoons is increasing. In 2019, Typhoon Hagibis caused catastrophic flooding and landslides across a wide area of Japan, resulting in enormous damage.

🥵

Record-Breaking Heat

In 2023, Japan recorded the hottest summer in history. Extremely high temperatures persisted in many regions, with heat stroke deaths becoming a serious social issue.

🌧️

Linear Precipitation Zones

A new weather phenomenon called "linear precipitation zones" has been repeatedly triggering severe flooding in western Japan, causing casualties and property damage each year.

2-2. The History and Future of Decarbonization Worldwide and in Japan

Progress Toward Decarbonization

🌐 Global Milestones

1997
Kyoto Protocol Adopted
The first international framework legally binding developed nations to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Japan committed to a 6% reduction from 1990 levels.
2015
Paris Agreement Signed
196 countries agreed to keep global temperature rise "well below 2°C" above pre-industrial levels, and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. Carbon neutrality by 2050 became a global goal.
2021
COP26 — Glasgow
The Glasgow Climate Pact called for phasing down unabated coal power and phasing out inefficient fossil fuel subsidies. Over 100 nations pledged to stop deforestation by 2030.
2023
COP28 — Dubai
For the first time, nations agreed to "transition away from fossil fuels." Tripling renewable energy capacity and doubling energy efficiency by 2030 were set as global targets.
2030
Critical Milestone
Global emissions must be cut by 43% from 2019 levels by 2030 to keep warming below 1.5°C. This decade is considered the most crucial period for climate action.

🗾 Japan's Decarbonization Path

2020
Carbon Neutrality Declaration
Prime Minister Suga declared Japan would achieve carbon neutrality — net-zero greenhouse gas emissions — by 2050. This marked a major shift in Japan's climate ambitions.
2021
46% Reduction Target by 2030
Japan raised its 2030 emissions reduction target from 26% to 46% (from 2013 levels). This significantly accelerated the pace of decarbonization required.
2022
GX Promotion Act Enacted
Japan passed legislation to mobilize over ¥150 trillion in public and private investment over 10 years to advance Green Transformation (GX), including biofuels, hydrogen, and carbon capture.
2025
Tokyo Metropolitan Government Subsidy
Tokyo launched a subsidy program supporting companies that switch to biofuels like SUSTEO, further accelerating adoption in the capital region.
2030
Target Year
All sectors of the Japanese economy — transportation, industry, buildings, and agriculture — must make significant emission cuts to meet the 46% reduction target.

2-4. Understanding Biofuel

What is Biofuel?

Biofuels are fuels derived from biological materials — plants, algae, used cooking oil, and other organic sources. Unlike fossil fuels, the carbon released when burning biofuels was originally absorbed from the atmosphere, making them largely carbon-neutral on a lifecycle basis.

🛢️ Fossil Fuels (Conventional Diesel)

Derived from ancient organic matter buried underground for millions of years. Burning fossil fuels releases carbon that has been locked away, increasing atmospheric CO₂.

  • Carbon locked underground for millions of years
  • Net addition of CO₂ to the atmosphere
  • Non-renewable, finite supply
  • Price volatile based on geopolitics

🌿 Biofuels (SUSTEO)

Derived from recently living biological materials. The CO₂ released when burned was recently absorbed from the atmosphere by the source plants, creating a near-closed carbon cycle.

  • Carbon recently absorbed from the atmosphere
  • Up to 90% net CO₂ reduction (lifecycle)
  • Renewable, sustainable feedstocks
  • Compatible with existing infrastructure
